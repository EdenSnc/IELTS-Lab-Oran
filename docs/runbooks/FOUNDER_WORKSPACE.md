# Founder workspace

Use the IELTS Lab mark as supplied, with its original proportions and details.
Start with the brand's crimson, charcoal and warm white. Let rounded surfaces,
quiet transitions and generous spacing carry the visual identity. Reserve
strong color for decisions, outcomes and errors. Avoid clutter, decorative
illustrations, emoji, startup slogans, glass effects and long explanatory copy.

Follow Apple Human Interface Guidelines for hierarchy, consistency, feedback,
accessibility and recovery. Use succinct labels and meaningful icons.
Use visuals, figures and touch-first controls where they shorten explanation.
Provide keyboard and text equivalents. Respect reduced motion. Keep layouts
usable on small phones and test long labels, empty states and failed saves.

The founder opens one workspace to review applicants and availability, manage
campaigns and posters, and reach payment and Speaking tools. Put the next
action in the first scan of each screen. Keep editing near the item, show
short save feedback and use confirmed Delete and Restore for removal. Keep printed
QR identifiers stable. Use a short, dismissible orientation in the workspace
and guide first use at the point of action.

Posters are saved print designs linked to a tracking location. The library
stores the original PNG or JPEG, placement and DPI. Moving a design to Trash
or purging it leaves the location and its scan history intact. Deleting campaigns,
tracking locations or applications moves records to Trash with a Restore action.
Trashing a campaign or location keeps its QR links at their current status.
Pausing a printed link is a separate action with an explicit confirmation.
Only empty campaigns and poster designs offer permanent deletion.
The editor adjusts QR width by one pixel and reserves the four-module quiet zone.
It can sample a light backing color, round the backing around that square,
or export over light, even poster artwork without a separate fill.

A campaign groups poster spots for an event, promotion or intake period.
The same campus can appear in multiple campaigns. Create one QR per physical
spot per campaign when you need separate measurements. Duplicate prints of a
QR share one history. Rename a spot in Tracking to keep its code and history.
The scan list shows new QR opens in Oran time. It links to submissions carrying
the individual scan token and retains the spot name at the time of the scan.
Older daily aggregates have no individual timestamps.

The workspace and its marketing/application APIs require ADMIN and MFA.
Examiner and learner tools use their own permissions.

## Next camp and online teaching

For new camps, publish only recurring class blocks the founder can actually
teach. Applicants select one offered block in Oran time. Until the timetable
is published, accept waitlist applications. A time choice is a preference
and enrolment still needs confirmation. Use factual availability: unpublished
or withdrawn times are unavailable. Use “Full” only for an actual seat limit.
Keep the selected schedule in the application so later timetable changes
preserve the original answer.
Publish or withdraw blocks in Founder Dashboard → Applications → Next-camp
timetable. Each block covers one group with the same hours on its selected
weekdays. Withdraw old blocks before opening a new timetable.
Editing a block replaces its ID. In-progress applications refresh their
choice, and submitted applications keep their original schedule snapshot.

Founder eligibility for the current partner-school venue, in Oran time:
Tuesday 08:00–12:00, Wednesday and Thursday 08:00–16:00. Friday and Saturday
are unavailable. Sunday and Monday are off because of university and travel.
These are private venue windows. Edit a draft down to the actual class hours
before publishing it to applicants.

The founder wants an affordable online IELTS course with small live groups and
hourly contract teachers. This is a teaching offer that can pilot with verified
lesson and practice material while the Cambridge extraction continues. Keep
the broader exam-library launch gate tied to extraction completeness. Start
with one paid group at a confirmed time. Use existing live-class tools first,
with IELTSLab for enrolment, materials, homework and feedback. Pay for teaching,
preparation and marking explicitly and measure those hours before scaling.

Planning scenario: six groups × eight learners × 5,000 DZD = 240,000 DZD gross
for one cycle. The 5,000 DZD is a modelling assumption carried over from the
introductory price. It is not a committed online fee. If all 32 hours per group
are instructor-led and the three teachers each take two groups, that is 192
instructor hours, including 128 contractor hours and 64 founder hours.
Subtract instructor time, admin, software, processing and support before
interpreting the margin. Engline Academy in Millenium, Oran is a reference for
online English groups, teacher selection and session links via Google Meet.
Its site mentions a coach with IELTS experience. A dedicated IELTS offer and
its unit economics have not been verified.

Use eight paying learners per online group in this planning model. For a
truly embedded one-group pilot, investigate Jitsi as a Service (JaaS) first.
Its developer tier lists 25 monthly active devices, so learners should use
their usual device when testing. Confirm billing and usage conditions before
opening a paid cohort. Use authenticated room pages, server-issued meeting
tokens, separate teacher and learner roles, and a no-recording default.
An IELTSLab lesson page with an external Meet link is a fallback. Personal
Meet calls with three or more participants have a 60-minute limit unless the
host account has an applicable subscription. Check account and regional
eligibility before relying on Google AI Pro's longer group meetings.
The existing LiveKit integration serves Speaking. Its free allowance of
5,000 participant minutes is about 9 hours with eight learners and a teacher.
One 32-hour group at full attendance uses 17,280 participant minutes.
